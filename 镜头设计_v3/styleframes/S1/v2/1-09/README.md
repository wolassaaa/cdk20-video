# 1-09 · ROAD MAP: first appearance (D, VO, 01:16–01:36)

Script (script_v3.3 1-09): "Every medicine walks the same road. Find a target. Find a hit, a first molecule that works on it. Improve it. Test it in cells, then animals. Then human trials: phase one, two, three. Then approval. Any step can fail."

Each option has two frames: `_full` (all six stations lit, attrition shown) and `_lit4` (stations 1–4 lit, 5–6 dim). The second frame shows the map can be reused at the ~12 later returns. On-screen text: "EVERY MEDICINE WALKS THE SAME ROAD", the six station names (01 TARGET · 02 HIT · 03 IMPROVE · 04 CELLS & ANIMALS · 05 HUMAN TRIALS / phase I·II·III · 06 APPROVAL) and "Any step can fail." Candidate counts (34 → 17 → 9 → 5 → 2 → 1) are illustrative, so every frame carries the ILLUSTRATIVE tag.

| Option | Idea | Why it fits / reuse logic | Draws on | Tool |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| opt1_neon-metro | The Vignelli 45°/90° metro line built as a real red neon tube on a black wall. Stations are bone discs. Failed candidates sit as grey beads on a ledge under each station, and the 34 starters are queued in red at the left. | The plainest diagram, made physical. For reuse, the neon is lit up to the current station and the rest is an unlit glass tube with dark discs. A real neon sign works the same way. | Massimo Vignelli (NYC subway diagram, 1972); Keith Sonnier and Dan Flavin (neon/fluorescent light as sculpture) | Blender Cycles + Remotion type |
| opt2_vessel-valves | A glossy red vessel snakes across an Yves-Klein-blue table, with six bone valve rings. The vessel narrows after every valve, so attrition is read through its width. | It is the strongest red-against-blue image. For reuse, the vessel past the current valve turns into a dark empty tube. | Tangle Creations' *Red Knot Chain* (glossy tubes, colour field); Yves Klein (IKB) | Blender + Remotion |
| opt3_light-gates | Six free-standing light frames on a black mirror floor against a blue horizon band. Red spheres thin out gate by gate and the drop-outs lie grey beside the path. The last gate (approval) is red. | It reads as an installation you walk through. For reuse, gates you have not reached are switched off (black frames), and the road line turns grey. | Dan Flavin (fluorescent frames); James Turrell (colour fields of light) | Blender + Remotion |
| opt4_glass-chambers | One long lab-glass tube split into six chambers. Red beads float in each chamber and fewer pass to the next; the failures settle grey on the floor of each chamber. A red thread runs along the axis. | This is "the pipeline", shown literally as a vessel. For reuse, the thread stops at chamber 4 and chambers 5–6 are empty glass. | Roni Horn (cast-glass volumes); the lab glassware vocabulary | Blender + Remotion |
| opt5_type-road | The station names are the road: extruded 3D letters laid flat like road markings, receding to a blue horizon. Red candidates thin out between words and the grey drop-outs roll onto the verge. | It is typographic and very readable up close, with numbers added in the overlay. For reuse, words not yet reached are dark paint and the centre line goes grey. | Lawrence Weiner (words as sculpture/place); road-marking typography | Blender + Remotion |
| opt6_chart-recorder | A chart-recorder paper strip rolls out of a Klein-blue instrument and curls on the table. A red pen trace steps down at each station: candidates still alive, on a log scale. | It is a scientific instrument metaphor in which the trace itself shows attrition. For reuse, the paper is printed only up to station 4 because the rest "hasn't come out yet", and the overlay lists 05/06 as NOT YET PRINTED. | Chart recorders / seismograph strips; Ryoji Ikeda's data aesthetics | Remotion (flat paper texture) → Blender (3D strip) + Remotion |
