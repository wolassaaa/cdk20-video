## Staging rules (v3; the director rejected v2 for these reasons)

v2 held one eye-level three-shot for 50 s. It read as a slide: the speaker did not stand out, the cards were small, the art's knee crop sat on the frame edge and the subtitle sat on the chests. What replaced it:

1. **Cut like a drama, from a shot list.** `SHOTS` in `Show3.jsx` is the only place staging lives: each shot has a kind (`wide` / `single` / `split`), who, which third the face sits on, a zoom range, a card, an entry (`cut` / `whip` / `snap` / `flash`) and small acting cues. Every `t0` is a word start from the timeline plus a few frames of offset, never a typed second. 32 shots in 50 s, longest 2.8 s. A new speaker's shot starts 5–6 frames before her first word.
2. **Sizes.** Wide (all three, scale 0.78–0.9, cut mid-thigh on the skirts, never at the knee line where the art ends) only to open and close. Everything else is a single: half-body at 1.24–1.31× and bust at 1.46–1.6×. The two sizes must differ by a quarter or more (1.24 vs 1.55): at 1.33 vs 1.5 the cut-in was invisible on the contact sheet and five seconds looked like one shot. Above 1.6× the 1536 px art goes soft, so no face close-ups. Inside one sentence, cut in along the axis once (half-body to bust) on the word where the number lands.
3. **Thirds.** With a card: face on one third (x 520 or 1400), card on the other side (780 px wide). Without: centred. Head top 46 px below the frame edge. The subtitle then lies on waist and skirt, never on a face or chest. Swap the side when the speaker changes so consecutive shots do not look alike.
4. **Interruptions.** Whip pan (about 5 frames out, 8 in, horizontal `feGaussianBlur` on the scene only, direction from the stage order Claude–GPT–DeepSeek) when a line cuts in on another. A snap zoom with a short shake for one stressed word ("YOU"). A hard cut with a shake for a one-word answer ("Nope.").
5. **One big hit only.** White flash + speed lines + fast push is used once, on "Plot twist!". Using it twice makes both weaker.
6. **Reactions.** Any sentence longer than about 2.5 s gets a listener cutaway (bust of the listener blinking, or a split screen of the two listeners) so no framing sits for more than 3 s. Cut to the listener while the other voice is still talking (mirrored image = looking the other way, a blink, then a squash-flip back just before she answers). For a fact-check, the second half of the line goes to a diagonal split screen with the person being corrected in the other half (eyes shut and sinking, or a double blink). A sigh is the body dropping 30 px and tilting 3°. Winks land on the last word of a brag.
7. **Stage.** Soft studio in layers that move by depth: gradient cyclorama, molecule lattice far back, tall softbox panels, two slow light shafts, two bokeh layers, a halo in the speaker's colour behind her, white + colour rim (`drop-shadow` on the PNG alpha). Closer shot = blurrier, larger background. In the wide shots the non-speakers are darkened, desaturated and slightly blurred.
8. **Card numbers.** Never show a counter before it starts. v2 printed the live counter value from the moment the card appeared, so the snake-toxin card read "0–0%" for four seconds (and the others "0 / 211", "0×", "0"). Now the number block stays closed until 0.45 s before the hit word; single integers count up for 0.4 s and stop on the word; ranges and tiny numbers ("80–100%", "2") appear as fixed values on the word. The caution line opens on the word that states the caution.
9. **Subtitles** one clause at a time, switched on that clause's first word (`CHUNKS`: clause text + index of its first Whisper word), 54 px, one line.
10. **Keep overlays mounted.** Card, subtitle, flash and speed lines stay in the tree and are hidden with opacity 0. When the card was unmounted at a cut, some rendered frames of the next shot still showed it (stale layer, different frames in each Chrome tab). Check card-less shots at every 3rd frame after any change.
11. **First frame of every shot must already be a picture.** Split-screen panels are in place from frame 0 and only the figures slide in; when the panels themselves slid in, the first frame was almost black. A line's last subtitle ends 0.1 s before the next speaker starts, so it never sits under the wrong face.
12. The camera lives in the composition (layout maths on the PNG layers); text overlays are separate layers and never scaled, so this is not a post-render zoom.

## Older rules (v2, still valid for mouths, credits and audio)

1. **Mouth**: the speaker switches open / talk_o / talk_a from the per-frame voice envelope (thresholds 0.14 and 0.48, smoothed; a change needs the previous state held 3 frames). The others cycle open / blink / wink on different periods; `react` in `lines.json` triggers wink (0.7 s) or wave (1.3 s) right after the line; entrance wave before the first line, goodbye wave after the last.
2. **Camera** (v2 only, replaced by the staging rules above): three.js perspective, fov 30; intro dolly; speaker change = small pan (0.1 of the speaker x); `punch` lines push to z 10.25 with a short shake; the speaker steps forward and hops (0.13, punch 0.26). Characters stand at x = -3.15 / 0 / +3.15 so nobody is cropped even at the tightest push. A wider stance (3.9) cropped the side characters; the 10-frame check found it.
3. **Cards** (`CARDS` in `Show2.jsx`, from the source table in `09_AI角色插段.md`): the card for a story appears on its naming word; the number counts up and lands on the `hit` word (`hit_t` from the timeline). Each card prints its source line and a caution line (binding only; company report not peer-reviewed; single toxins; early leads).
4. **Subtitles**: burned in at the bottom, Instrument Serif (`@remotion/google-fonts/InstrumentSerif`), speaker name in the character colour. In v2 the cards sat at the top; in v3 they sit on the third opposite the speaker. The credit line "Illustration. Characters adapted from fan designs by ZipZipPipe (Bilibili) and Shangshan Wuxing (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0)." sits bottom right on a dark pill so it stays readable over the white apron.
5. **Stage** (v2 only, replaced by the staging rules above): dark theatre, hex lattice backdrop, bokeh, one beam and a colour rim glow per character, sparkles on wink. No flat gradient backdrop.
6. Audio is added in the composition with `<Audio src={staticFile("m/mix.wav")} />`; every event time comes from `timeline_v2.json`; no time is hard-coded.

## Render (1080p60, about 8 minutes for 3002 frames in v3)

```
cd 镜头设计_v2/tests/T4_remotion
npx remotion render src/v3/mascots/entry.jsx MascotShow3 $CDK20_WORK/mascots/frames5 --sequence --image-format=jpeg --jpeg-quality=95 --gl=angle --concurrency=2 --public-dir=$CDK20_WORK/mascots/pub
<ffmpeg with libx264> -framerate 60 -i frames5/element-%04d.jpeg -i pub/m/mix.wav -c:v libx264 -preset slow -crf 15 -pix_fmt yuv420p -c:a aac -b:a 192k -shortest out.mp4
```

- Rendering straight to mp4 crashed here with `write EOF` after a few dozen frames, so render a frame sequence and then encode. Remotion's bundled ffmpeg cannot write output files on this machine; use the CipherTalk ffmpeg-static build (`D:/software/tools/CipherTalk/resources/app.asar.unpacked/node_modules/ffmpeg-static/ffmpeg.exe`). TouchDesigner's ffmpeg has no libx264.
- The guard hook blocks `rm -rf` on paths under the Remotion project folder; write to a new frame folder instead of deleting.
- Deliver to `导演检查_<date>/11_AI角色插段.mp4`; keep the old versions as `_v1`, `_v2`.

## Check list (every 10th frame, 301 frames, contact sheets of 30)

- `ffmpeg -start_number N -framerate 60 -i frames/element-%04d.jpeg -vf "select='not(mod(n,10))',scale=480:-1,tile=5x6" -frames:v 1 sheet.jpg`
- Camera: the framing changes at every line and nothing holds longer than 4 s; look at the frame before, the first frame and the frame after every cut (`SHOTS[i].f0`) and the frames around every hit word, not only every 10th frame. Also measure all frames: mean luma under 20 = blank frame, identical neighbours = frozen frame.
- Characters never cropped at a joint or at the art's knee line; side characters fully inside the wide shots, waving hands included; mouth follows the voice; no blank frame (only frame 0 is a deliberate cream flash for the light sweep); subtitle at the bottom and card at the top never overlap; source and credit lines readable; card number lands on its hit word; the last spoken word ends before the segment ends.
